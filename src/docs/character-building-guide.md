# 🧬 Character Building Guide: Backstory, Personality & Playstyle

Welcome, new roleplayer! 👋 A strong character is the difference between people remembering your name and people forgetting you existed after one server restart. This guide walks you through **building a character that feels real** — step by step. 📖

---

## 1️⃣ Start With a Concept, Not a Costume

🔹 A concept is the one-sentence idea your whole character is built around. Skins, cars, and clothing come *after* this — not before.

✔️ Good concepts:

- "A mechanic who owes money to the wrong crew and is trying to work it off quietly"
- "An ex-cop turned taxi driver who still can't mind their own business"
- "A small-time hustler saving up to open a legit business"

❌ Not a concept:

- "Guy with cool car and mask" — that's an outfit, not a character
- Copy-pasting a popular streamer's character 1:1

> 💡 **Rule of thumb:** if you can't explain your character in one sentence to a stranger, the concept isn't locked in yet.

---

## 2️⃣ Build a Believable Backstory

### 📜 What it needs

🔹 Your backstory explains **why** your character behaves the way they do today. It doesn't need to be long — it needs to be *consistent*.

✔️ Cover at minimum:

- Where they're from and why they ended up in the city
- What they do (or did) for money
- One relationship that matters to them (family, mentor, rival, partner)
- One event that shaped their current attitude (a loss, a debt, a lucky break)

⚠️ Common mistakes:

- Writing a backstory nobody in-game will ever find out (keep some secret — but not *all* of it)
- Giving your character trauma/backstory that you then never actually roleplay
- Making your backstory so tragic and overpowered it reads like a movie trailer instead of a person

---

## 3️⃣ Define Personality Traits (Not Just "Chill" or "Toxic")

🔹 Real personalities are made of **contradictions**, not one adjective. Pick 2–3 core traits and one flaw.

✔️ Example combo:

- Loyal to friends, but quick to assume the worst about strangers
- Confident in public, genuinely anxious about being caught lying
- Generous with money, terrible at saying no to bad deals

📝 Ask yourself:

- How does my character react under pressure — freeze, fight, or talk their way out?
- What's one thing my character is proud of?
- What's one thing my character is embarrassed about and would lie about?

> 💡 A flaw isn't just for flavor — it should occasionally get your character *into* trouble in RP, not just described in a bio nobody reads.

---

## 4️⃣ Speech Patterns & Mannerisms

🔹 How your character talks is often the first thing other players notice — before your outfit, before your car.

✔️ Decide on:

- Vocabulary level (street slang vs. formal vs. mixed)
- Catchphrases or verbal tics (used sparingly — don't overdo it)
- How they address strangers vs. friends vs. authority
- Physical habits: fidgeting, specific gestures, a distinct laugh

⚠️ Avoid:

- Switching your speech style mid-scene because it's easier for you (OOC convenience) instead of what's IC
- Copying another well-known character's exact mannerisms

---

## 5️⃣ Goals: Short-Term, Long-Term, and Secret

🔹 A character with nothing to *want* has nothing to roleplay toward. Give yours layered goals.

| Type       | Example                                      | Purpose                           |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Short-term | Pay off this week's debt                     | Gives you something to do today   |
| Long-term  | Own a legit garage business                  | Gives your arc direction          |
| Secret     | Find out who set them up years ago           | Gives other players a reason to RP with you long-term |

> 💡 Secret goals are what make other players *want* to plot with you — don't reveal everything on day one.

---

## 6️⃣ Relationships Make the Character

🔹 A character who only interacts with strangers stays shallow. Build a small circle early:

✔️ Aim for at least:

- One ally (someone who has your back)
- One rival or unresolved tension (doesn't have to be violent — could be a rivalry, an old debt, a falling-out)
- One mentor or authority figure your character respects or resents

⚠️ Don't force relationships OOC ("we're best friends because we're friends in Discord") — build them **through actual RP scenes**, not assumption.

---

## 7️⃣ Staying In Character (Consistency Over Time)

🔹 The hardest part of character building isn't creating the character — it's staying consistent with them weeks later.

✔️ Tips:

- Keep a short private note (name, core traits, current goals, key relationships) you can re-read before sessions
- Let your character grow from what happens IC — a robbery gone wrong, a friendship, a betrayal — don't reset them back to "default" every session
- If your character changes drastically, make sure it happened through roleplay, not just because you felt like it OOC

> ☠️ Reminder: playing wildly out of your established character just to win a scene or avoid a consequence can cross into **FailRP** — see the Roleplay Basics guide for what that means.

---

## ✅ Character Building Checklist

- [ ] One-sentence concept
- [ ] Backstory: origin, job, key relationship, shaping event
- [ ] 2–3 personality traits + 1 flaw
- [ ] Speech pattern / mannerisms
- [ ] Short-term goal
- [ ] Long-term goal
- [ ] Secret goal
- [ ] At least one ally and one rival/tension
- [ ] A private note you can re-read to stay consistent

---

## 💡 Beginner's Golden Rule

> **"Would this character actually do this, or am I doing this because it's convenient for me?"**
> If it's the second one — that's you talking, not your character. Stay true to who you built, and other players will remember your name. 🎉
